## Unit 1 British Geography

### 1. Geography and Location

核心句：The United Kingdom is made up of four constituent countries: England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

- 全称：the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland。
- Great Britain 指 England, Scotland and Wales，不包括 Northern Ireland。
- The UK 指 Great Britain 加 Northern Ireland。
- England 不是 Britain，也不是 the UK。考试中常考这个混淆点。
- British Isles 是地理概念，范围比 UK 大，包括 Great Britain, Ireland and many smaller islands。
- England 面积和人口最大，政治、经济、文化影响力最强，因此外国人常把 England 误当作 Britain/UK。
- London 是英国政治、商业、金融、文化和媒体中心，对全国有很强支配力。
- Scotland 是第二大组成部分，地形更崎岖，有 Highlands and Lowlands，首府 Edinburgh，最大城市 Glasgow。
- Wales 位于大不列颠西部，首府 Cardiff，保留 Welsh language，是 Welsh identity 的重要标志。
- Northern Ireland 位于爱尔兰岛东北部，是 UK 的一部分，但历史、宗教和身份问题更复杂。

速记：

- UK = England + Scotland + Wales + Northern Ireland
- Great Britain = England + Scotland + Wales
- England = one part, not the whole country

### 2. British Identity

核心句：British identity is not simple; it is shaped by national, regional, class, racial and imperial histories.

- 英国是一个统一国家，有共同的 passport, government and sovereignty。
- 但英国人身份是多层的：British, English, Scottish, Welsh, Irish/Northern Irish 等身份并存。
- Scottish identity 很强，因为 Scotland 曾长期作为独立王国存在，1707 年才与 England 组成 Union。
- Wales 的身份感主要来自 Welsh language, local culture and historical memory。
- England 在文化和经济上占优势，所以 English identity 有时反而被 British identity 覆盖。
- 英国是 multiracial society，战后来自 Commonwealth countries 的移民改变了人口结构。
- Class structure 仍然明显，教育、口音、职业、报纸和生活方式都可能体现阶级差异。
- Regional differences 也重要，如 north/south England, highland/lowland Scotland, London/provinces。
- London dominance 是英国身份和地区不平衡的重要背景。

可考问法：

- Why is British identity complicated?
- What is the difference between England, Great Britain and the UK?
- Why do Scottish and Welsh people not simply call themselves English?

## Unit 2 British History

### 1. Norman Conquest

核心句：The Norman Conquest in 1066 changed England's ruling class, language, land system and political development.

- 时间：1066。
- 人物：William of Normandy / William the Conqueror。
- 事件：Battle of Hastings 中击败 Anglo-Saxon king Harold，成为 William I of England。
- 意义：这是英国本土最后一次被外来军队成功征服。
- Normans 没有大规模移民，而是输入 ruling class。
- 之后三百年形成 Norman French-speaking aristocracy ruling a largely Saxon English-speaking population。
- 影响：
  - 加强中央王权和封建土地制度。
  - 法语影响英语词汇，尤其是法律、政府、贵族和饮食词汇。
  - 城堡和军事控制加强，如 Tower of London。
  - 促成 England 更统一的王权结构。

### 2. The Hundred Years' War with France

核心句：The Hundred Years' War helped weaken feudalism and strengthen national consciousness in England and France.

- 时间：1337-1453。
- 双方：England and France。
- 原因：
  - 英王对法国王位的继承要求。
  - 英国在法国领地和贸易利益的冲突。
  - 封建领地与民族国家形成之间的矛盾。
- 重要结果：
  - England 最终失去大部分在法国的领地。
  - France national identity strengthened。
  - England 也逐渐把注意力转回国内。
  - 战争费用推动 Parliament 在 taxation 上更重要。
- 可联系：longbow, national monarchy, decline of feudal knights。

### 3. The Wars of the Roses

核心句：The Wars of the Roses were dynastic civil wars that ended medieval instability and opened the Tudor age.

- 时间：考试常写 1455-1485；更严格的历史分期常写 1455-1487，因为 1487 Battle of Stoke Field 后 Yorkist resistance 才基本结束。
- 双方：House of Lancaster and House of York。
- 象征：Lancaster = red rose; York = white rose。
- 性质：争夺王位的贵族内战。
- 关键转折：1485 Battle of Bosworth，Henry Tudor 击败 Richard III，成为 Henry VII。
- 影响：
  - Tudor dynasty 开始。
  - 贵族力量削弱，王权加强。
  - 为更集中的 national monarchy 铺路。

### 4. Glorious Revolution

核心句：The Glorious Revolution established the supremacy of Parliament over the Crown and set Britain on the path toward constitutional monarchy.

- 时间：1688-1689。
- 背景：James II 试图不经 Parliament 同意而统治，引发政治和宗教危机。
- 过程：Parliament 邀请 William of Orange and Mary 取代 James II。
- 1689：William and Mary 接受王位。
- Bill of Rights 1689：
  - 限制 royal power。
  - Parliament 控制 taxation, finance and army。
  - 王权不得随意暂停法律或绕过 Parliament。
- 影响：
  - Parliament supremacy over the Crown。
  - constitutional monarchy 形成。
  - 后来 cabinet government and Prime Minister 制度发展起来。

### 5. Industrial Revolution

核心句：Britain was the first country to industrialise, which made it economically powerful but also produced class, urban and social problems.

- 时间：18-19 世纪。
- 英国率先工业化的条件：
  - coal and iron resources。
  - capital from trade and empire。
  - technological inventions such as steam power。
  - large market and transport improvements。
  - political stability and property protection。
- 主要行业：textiles, coal, iron, steam engines, railways。
- 影响：
  - factory system 取代传统手工业。
  - urbanization 加速，Manchester 等工业城市兴起。
  - middle class and working class 壮大。
  - British economy became dominant in the world by the 19th century。
  - 同时带来 poverty, child labour, bad working conditions and class conflict。

## Unit 3 British Government and Economy

### 1. The Monarchy: Constitutional Monarchy

核心句：The British monarch reigns but does not rule.

- 英国是 parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy。
- Monarch 是 Head of State，但实际政治权力由 elected government 行使。
- 现代 monarch 的作用：
  - symbolise the tradition and unity of the British state。
  - formally appoint the Prime Minister。
  - give Royal Assent to bills。
  - open Parliament。
  - meet the Prime Minister regularly。
  - represent the nation ceremonially。
- 真正 governing power 在 Prime Minister, Cabinet and Parliament。
- monarchy 的争议：
  - 支持者认为它提供传统、稳定、国家象征和非党派元首。
  - 批评者认为它 hereditary, costly and undemocratic。

答题句：

- Britain keeps the monarchy, but the monarch's political power is limited by constitutional principles and conventions.

### 2. Parliament: House of Commons and House of Lords

核心句：Parliament is supreme in the British state, and the House of Commons is the real centre of political power.

- Strictly speaking, Parliament = the monarch + the House of Lords + the House of Commons。
- Parliament 的主要职能：
  - pass laws。
  - vote taxation and public expenditure。
  - scrutinise government。
  - debate national issues。
  - represent the electorate。
- Parliamentary sovereignty：
  - Parliament has unlimited legal authority。
  - It can make, change or repeal any law。
  - No other body can override an Act of Parliament in the traditional doctrine。
- House of Commons：
  - MPs are elected by voters。
  - about 650 MPs。
  - controls legislation, finance and government accountability。
  - party with most seats normally forms government。
- House of Lords：
  - unelected upper chamber。
  - includes Lords Spiritual and life peers; older textbooks may also mention remaining hereditary peers。
  - revises and delays legislation but is weaker than Commons。
  - House of Lords Act 1999 greatly reduced hereditary peers; the House of Lords (Hereditary Peers) Act 2026 removed the remaining hereditary peers from sitting by hereditary right。
  - Its judicial function was removed with the creation of the UK Supreme Court.

### 3. Party Politics: General Election and Two-Party System

核心句：British government is formed through parliamentary elections, not by directly electing the Prime Minister.

- General election 选的是 MPs。
- 获得 House of Commons 多数席位的政党组成 government。
- 该党 leader 通常成为 Prime Minister。
- 主要政党：
  - Conservative Party: traditionally centre-right, market-oriented, linked with business and tradition。
  - Labour Party: traditionally centre-left, linked with working class, trade unions and welfare。
  - Liberal Democrats and smaller parties 也影响政治，尤其在 hung parliament 中。
- Opposition：
  - 最大反对党形成 official opposition。
  - Shadow Cabinet 监督对应的 government ministers。

### 4. Prime Minister and Evolution of Parliament

核心句：The Prime Minister grew out of the cabinet system as Parliament became stronger than the monarch.

- Cabinet 最初是 monarch 与重要 parliamentarians 协商的机构。
- George I 不熟悉英语政治，逐渐把主持 cabinet meetings 的工作交给主要 minister。
- Prime Minister 逐渐成为 national leader and head of government。
- 1832 Reform Act 推动 House of Commons 更具代表性，现代政党政治发展。
- PM 的主要权力：
  - select and lead the Cabinet。
  - lead the governing party。
  - control government policy agenda。
  - represent the UK domestically and internationally。
- Collective cabinet responsibility：
  - Cabinet ministers publicly support government decisions。
  - 若不能支持，应 resign。
  - Cabinet 对 Parliament 集体负责。

### 5. British Economy

核心句：The British economy moved from early industrial dominance to post-war relative decline, then toward a service-dominated economy.

- Britain was the first country to industrialise。
- 1945 后英国经济仍绝对增长，但相对其他国家 decline。
- Relative decline 的原因：
  - war damage and military expenditure。
  - loss of empire and markets。
  - low domestic industrial investment。
  - high overseas investment。
  - competition from Germany, Japan and later other economies。
  - labour-management problems and older industrial structure。
- Thatcherism in the 1980s：
  - monetarism。
  - privatisation。
  - tax reduction。
  - cutting public spending and welfare。
  - weakening trade unions。
  - encouraging market competition。
- Thatcherism 的影响：
  - inflation controlled and some sectors became more efficient。
  - unemployment and social inequality increased。
  - coal and manufacturing declined sharply。
  - service and financial sectors expanded。
- 2008 financial crisis：
  - Britain was badly hit because finance was a large part of the economy。
  - government rescued banks and tried to restore lending and confidence。
- Three sectors：
  - primary: agriculture, fishing, mining。
  - secondary: manufacturing and construction。
  - tertiary: services。
- Service industries dominate modern UK GDP。
- The City of London：
  - one of the world's leading financial centres。
  - includes London Stock Exchange, Lloyd's of London, foreign banks and financial services。
  - finance is a key part of Britain's service exports。
- Manufacturing today is smaller but strong in high-value areas such as pharmaceuticals, aerospace, design, technology and innovation。

## Unit 4 British Literature

### 1. History or Timeline

核心句：British literature develops from Renaissance drama to Romantic poetry, 19th-century novels, Modernism and contemporary fiction.

- Renaissance / Elizabethan Age：
  - drama flourished。
  - major writers: Christopher Marlowe, Ben Jonson, William Shakespeare。
- 18th century：
  - rise of the novel and satire。
  - Daniel Defoe is often associated with the early English novel。
  - Jonathan Swift and Alexander Pope are important satirical/classical writers。
- Romantic Period：
  - roughly late 18th to early 19th century; Lyrical Ballads appeared in 1798 and is often used as a starting point。
  - emphasis on imagination, emotion, nature, individual freedom and rebellion against cold rationalism。
  - major poets: Wordsworth, Coleridge, Byron, Shelley, Keats。
- 19th-century novel：
  - Jane Austen belongs to the Georgian/Regency context, while the Bronte sisters, Charles Dickens and Thomas Hardy are commonly linked with the Victorian novel。
  - focus on manners, morality, class, industrial society and social criticism。
- Modernism：
  - experimentation with form, consciousness and uncertainty。
  - Virginia Woolf and Joseph Conrad are important examples in the textbook context。
- Contemporary literature：
  - Booker Prize, postcolonial writers and global British identity become important。

### 2. Elizabethan Drama: William Shakespeare

核心句：Shakespeare is the greatest figure of Elizabethan drama and one of the most influential writers in world literature.

- Life: 1564-1616, from Stratford-upon-Avon。
- Worked with the theatre world in London; associated with the Globe Theatre。
- Main categories of plays:
  - tragedies: Hamlet, Othello, King Lear, Macbeth。
  - comedies: A Midsummer Night's Dream, Twelfth Night, As You Like It。
  - histories: Henry IV, Henry V, Richard III。
- Features:
  - deep understanding of human nature。
  - rich language and imagery。
  - complex characters。
  - universal themes: ambition, love, jealousy, power, revenge, fate, moral choice。
- Importance:
  - represents the peak of English Renaissance drama。
  - greatly enriched the English language。
  - still performed and studied worldwide。

### 3. Romanticism

核心句：Romantic literature values imagination, emotion, nature, individuality and freedom.

- Time: roughly late 18th to early 19th century。
- Background:
  - reaction against industrialization, urbanization and rationalism。
  - writers sought spiritual refuge in nature and imagination。
- First generation:
  - William Wordsworth: nature, ordinary life, plain language。
  - Samuel Taylor Coleridge: imagination, supernatural, lyrical power。
  - Lyrical Ballads, first published in 1798, is often seen as a key beginning of English Romanticism。
- Second generation:
  - Lord Byron: rebellious, heroic, exile, freedom。
  - Percy Bysshe Shelley: idealism, revolution, lyric beauty。
  - John Keats: beauty, art, sensuous imagery, short but brilliant life。
- Key features:
  - nature as moral and spiritual teacher。
  - emphasis on subjective feeling。
  - sympathy for the individual and the oppressed。
  - imagination over strict classical rules。

### 4. 19th-Century Novel: Charles Dickens

核心句：Charles Dickens is the greatest British storyteller of the 19th century and a powerful social critic.

- Dickens wrote in the context of industrial Britain and urban poverty。
- He cared about the suffering of the poor, children, debtors and workers。
- Major works:
  - Oliver Twist
  - David Copperfield
  - Great Expectations
  - A Tale of Two Cities
  - Hard Times
  - Bleak House
- Features:
  - vivid characters。
  - memorable plots。
  - humour and sentiment。
  - criticism of social injustice。
  - attention to child labour, workhouses, courts, industrial cities and class inequality。
- Exam sentence:
  - Dickens is both a great novelist and a social critic because his novels expose the problems of Victorian society while creating unforgettable characters.

## Unit 5 British Education

### 1. Educational System

核心句：British education provides literacy and socialisation, but it also reflects class differences.

- Purpose:
  - teach literacy and numeracy。
  - socialise children into society。
  - prepare citizens and workers。
- Class issue:
  - education is closely connected with social class。
  - "old school tie" means elite school networks that help careers and social status。
  - public schools such as Eton and Harrow are actually private, fee-paying independent schools。
- Church influence:
  - many early schools were set up by churches。
  - religious education and school prayers historically mattered。
  - faith schools still exist。
- 1944 Education Act:
  - free secondary education。
  - compulsory education expanded。
  - tripartite system: grammar schools, secondary modern schools, technical schools。
  - 11-plus exam selected pupils at age 11。
- Grammar schools:
  - selected academically able pupils。
  - main route to university for many who could not afford private education。
- Comprehensive schools:
  - introduced widely in the late 1960s。
  - admitted pupils without selection by academic ability。
  - aimed to reduce inequality and replace the tripartite division。
- National Curriculum:
  - introduced under Conservative reforms。
  - set national standards and core subjects。
- Modern diversity of schools:
  - state schools, comprehensive schools, grammar schools, faith schools, specialist schools, academies, free schools and independent schools。
- Main stages:
  - early years。
  - primary education。
  - secondary education。
  - further education。
  - higher education。
- Exams:
  - GCSE at about age 16。
  - A-levels after sixth form, usually for university entrance。
  - vocational alternatives also exist。

### 2. Higher Education

核心句：British higher education has expanded from an elite system to a mass system, while still showing hierarchy and class influence.

- Old universities:
  - Oxford and Cambridge are the oldest and most prestigious。
  - "Oxbridge" is often linked with elite networks。
- Expansion:
  - redbrick universities grew in industrial cities。
  - new universities expanded in the 1960s。
  - in 1992, polytechnics became universities, greatly increasing the number of universities。
- Open University:
  - established in 1969。
  - offers distance learning。
  - allows people without traditional qualifications to study for degrees。
  - supports mature students and lifelong learning。
- Tuition fees:
  - from 1998, upfront tuition fees were introduced for many full-time undergraduates, especially in England and Wales; devolved systems later differed。
  - student loans became part of the system。
- Trends:
  - participation increased greatly; nearly half of young people entered higher education by the mid-2010s according to the textbook。
  - access widened for mature and non-traditional students。
  - British universities attract many international students。

## Unit 6 American Geography

### 1. Land

核心句：The United States is geographically vast and regionally diverse, and its land has shaped settlement, economy and identity.

- The US has 50 states:
  - 48 contiguous states。
  - Alaska。
  - Hawaii。
- Alaska is the largest state and was purchased from Russia in 1867。
- Hawaii became a state in 1959 and lies in the Pacific。
- Important physical features:
  - Atlantic Ocean in the east。
  - Pacific Ocean in the west。
  - Gulf of Mexico in the south。
  - Appalachian Mountains in the east。
  - Rocky Mountains in the west。
  - Mississippi River and Missouri River as major river systems。
  - Great Lakes in the north。
- Major regions:
  - Northeast: New England and Mid-Atlantic; old settlement, industry, finance, education。
  - Midwest: agriculture, industry, Mississippi/Missouri rivers, "heartland" image。
  - South: plantation/slavery history, Civil War memory, Gulf economy, strong regional identity。
  - West: frontier history, Rocky Mountains, deserts, California and Pacific influence。
  - Pacific Northwest: forests, rivers, Native American history, environmental culture。
  - Southwest: desert landscapes, Hispanic and Native American influences。
- Land and economy:
  - farming remains important in many regions。
  - fishing matters on Pacific, Atlantic and Gulf coasts。
  - geography influences population distribution, transport and regional culture。

### 2. People: Immigrants

核心句：The United States is a nation of immigrants, but immigration has always involved both opportunity and conflict.

- Diversity is the most important concept for understanding the American people。
- The US has long been described as a melting pot, but salad bowl may be more accurate because groups often keep distinct identities。
- Native Americans / American Indians lived in North America before European arrival。
- Major waves of immigration:
  - 17th-18th centuries: mainly English and other Northern Europeans; Africans were also brought by force as slaves。
  - 19th century: Germans, Irish, Scandinavians and others; Irish immigration surged during the Potato Famine。
  - mid-19th century: Chinese immigrants came especially during the Gold Rush and railroad building。
  - late 19th and early 20th centuries: Southern and Eastern Europeans increased。
  - after 1945: refugees and immigrants from war-torn Europe and other regions。
  - after 1965: Hart-Celler Act removed national-origin quotas and opened more immigration from Asia, Latin America and elsewhere。
- Restrictive immigration:
  - Chinese Exclusion Act restricted Chinese immigration。
  - 1920s quota laws favoured Northern and Western Europe。
- Ethnic groups and issues:
  - Hispanic/Latino Americans are the largest ethnic minority group; racial categories are counted separately in US statistics。
  - African Americans include descendants of enslaved Africans and later immigrants from Africa and the Caribbean。
  - Asian Americans are sometimes stereotyped as a "model minority", but this term hides inequality and discrimination。
  - immigration debates often involve race, religion, jobs, borders and national identity。
- Religion:
  - Christianity remains the largest tradition, but religious diversity is significant。
  - freedom of religion is a core American principle。

## Unit 7 American History

### 1. American Beginnings: Early Settlers

核心句：Early American society was shaped by Native peoples, European colonisation, African slavery and Puritan culture.

- Before Europeans:
  - Native Americans lived across North America with diverse cultures shaped by land and climate。
  - The textbook also mentions major pre-Columbian civilizations such as the Aztecs, Incas and Mayas in the broader Americas。
- Jamestown:
  - founded in Virginia in 1607。
  - first permanent English settlement in North America。
  - tobacco helped the colony survive economically。
  - House of Burgesses in 1619 was an early representative assembly。
  - 1619 marks the first documented arrival of forcibly captured Africans in English North America; it is often treated as the beginning of African slavery there, though their exact legal status at the time is debated and hereditary racial slavery developed later。
- Puritan New England:
  - Puritans wanted to purify the Church of England。
  - They believed in predestination, calling, hard work, moral discipline and Bible reading。
  - Mayflower sailed in 1620。
  - Pilgrims signed the Mayflower Compact before landing, creating a civil government for the common good。
  - They settled at Plymouth, Massachusetts。
  - A larger Puritan colony developed around Boston in 1630。
- Puritan influence on American culture:
  - belief in hard work and self-discipline。
  - emphasis on education and literacy because everyone should read the Bible。
  - moral seriousness, sometimes intolerant moralism。
  - idea of building a "city upon a hill"。
  - tradition of local self-government and community responsibility。
- Roger Williams:
  - argued for separation of church and state。
  - associated with religious freedom in Rhode Island。

### 2. American Revolution: Cause, Process and Impact

核心句：The American Revolution began as a conflict over rights and taxation and ended by creating an independent republic.

Causes:

- After the French and Indian War, Britain wanted the colonies to pay part of imperial costs。
- Colonists opposed taxes imposed without colonial representation in Parliament。
- Key slogan: no taxation without representation。
- British policies such as the Stamp Act, Townshend Acts, Tea Act and Intolerable Acts increased resistance。
- Colonists feared British troops and laws would threaten their civil liberties。

Process:

- First Continental Congress met in Philadelphia in 1774。
- Fighting began at Lexington and Concord in 1775。
- Second Continental Congress took charge of the war effort。
- Declaration of Independence was adopted on July 4, 1776。
- Thomas Jefferson was the chief author。
- France later supported the American side。
- Treaty of Paris 1783 recognized American independence。

Impact:

- The 13 colonies became the United States of America。
- Republican government and popular sovereignty became central ideals。
- Declaration of Independence expressed natural rights, equality and government by consent。
- The Revolution inspired later democratic movements。
- Contradiction remained: slavery, Native dispossession and women's exclusion continued.

### 3. Civil War

核心句：The Civil War preserved the Union and destroyed slavery, but it did not end racial conflict.

- Time: 1861-1865。
- Sides:
  - North / Union。
  - South / Confederacy。
- Main causes:
  - slavery and its expansion into new territories。
  - conflict between federal power and states' rights。
  - economic and social differences between industrial North and plantation South。
  - election of Abraham Lincoln in 1860 led Southern states to secede。
- Key events:
  - Confederacy formed by Southern states。
  - Emancipation Proclamation in 1863 changed the war into a fight against slavery。
  - Gettysburg was a major turning point。
  - South surrendered in 1865。
- Results:
  - Union preserved。
  - slavery abolished by the 13th Amendment。
  - 14th Amendment guaranteed citizenship and equal protection。
  - 15th Amendment protected Black men's voting rights。
  - Reconstruction tried to rebuild the South but racial inequality continued。

## Unit 8 American Government and Economy

### 1. US Constitution

核心句：The US Constitution created a federal system with a strong but limited central government.

- Before the Constitution, the Articles of Confederation created a weak national government。
- Problems under Articles:
  - Congress could not force states to obey。
  - Congress could not effectively collect taxes。
  - states competed with each other。
- Constitutional Convention:
  - held in Philadelphia in 1787。
  - created a new Constitution。
- Main principles:
  - federalism: power divided between national and state governments。
  - separation of powers: legislative, executive and judicial branches。
  - checks and balances。
  - written constitution as supreme law。
  - limited government。
- Preamble states broad purposes such as forming a more perfect union and securing liberty。
- 10th Amendment reserves powers not given to the federal government to the states or the people。

### 2. Checks and Balances / Separation of Powers

核心句：Checks and balances prevent any one branch from taking over the government.

- Legislative branch:
  - Congress = Senate + House of Representatives。
  - makes laws。
  - levies taxes。
  - controls federal spending。
  - can override presidential veto by a supermajority。
  - Senate confirms many appointments and gives advice and consent on treaties。
- Executive branch:
  - President, Vice President and departments。
  - enforces laws。
  - conducts foreign relations。
  - commands armed forces。
  - can veto bills。
  - appoints federal judges and officials, subject to Senate confirmation。
- Judicial branch:
  - headed by Supreme Court。
  - interprets laws and Constitution。
  - judicial review allows courts to declare laws or executive actions unconstitutional。
- Examples:
  - President vetoes Congress; Congress can override。
  - Supreme Court can strike down unconstitutional laws。
  - Senate can reject presidential appointments。
  - The House can impeach federal officials; the Senate tries impeachment cases。

### 3. Bill of Rights

核心句：The Bill of Rights is the first ten amendments to the Constitution and protects individual liberties from government interference.

- Added in 1791。
- Purpose: reassure citizens that the new federal government would not violate basic rights。
- Major rights:
  - freedom of religion。
  - freedom of speech。
  - freedom of the press。
  - freedom of assembly and petition。
  - protection against unreasonable searches。
  - due process。
  - right to a speedy and public trial。
  - right to jury trial。
  - protection against cruel and unusual punishment。
- Importance:
  - foundation of American civil liberties。
  - limits government power。
  - often used in Supreme Court cases。

### 4. US Presidential Election

核心句：The US president is chosen through an indirect election using the Electoral College.

- Nomination stage:
  - primary elections and caucuses。
  - party conventions formally nominate candidates。
- General election:
  - held in November。
  - voters choose electors, not the president directly。
- Electoral College:
  - total 538 electoral votes。
  - candidate needs at least 270 to win。
  - electors cast votes for President and Vice President。
- Controversy:
  - a candidate can win the Electoral College while losing the popular vote。
  - 2016 election is a textbook example: Donald Trump won electoral votes while Hillary Clinton won more popular votes。
- Supporters say it balances state interests; critics say it can distort popular democracy。

### 5. Two-Party System

核心句：The United States has a two-party system dominated by the Democratic Party and the Republican Party.

- Democratic Party:
  - roots in the Democratic-Republican Party of the 1790s, but the modern Democratic Party is usually dated to 1828。
  - symbol: donkey。
  - generally more liberal in modern US politics。
  - supports a more active role for government in welfare, regulation and social programs。
  - associated with New Deal policies under Franklin D. Roosevelt。
- Republican Party:
  - formed in the 1850s。
  - symbol: elephant。
  - originally opposed the expansion of slavery into new states。
  - generally more conservative in modern politics。
  - supports free enterprise, lower taxes and smaller government。
- Why two parties dominate:
  - winner-take-all elections。
  - Electoral College。
  - ballot access and campaign finance structures。
  - voters often see third parties as unable to win。

### 6. American Economy

核心句：The US economy grew through industrialization, a large internal market, entrepreneurship and later the shift from manufacturing to services and high technology.

- Early growth:
  - industrialization after independence。
  - textile manufacturing, cotton economy and trade expansion。
  - interchangeable parts improved factory production。
- Key features:
  - large internal market。
  - abundant resources。
  - immigration supplied labour。
  - entrepreneurship and relatively free market institutions encouraged growth。
- Agriculture:
  - still vital despite small share of employment。
  - modern American agriculture is agribusiness: farm production plus processing, machinery, finance, transport and marketing。
  - high productivity keeps food prices low and supports exports。
  - problems include debt, market risk, environmental pressure and corporate concentration。
- Modern shift:
  - manufacturing employment has declined。
  - high-tech industries and services have grown。
  - service industries dominate the economy。
- 2007-2008 financial crisis:
  - involved housing markets, financial institutions and stock markets。
  - exposed weaknesses in free-market and financial policy。
  - recovery was slow but steady in the textbook account。

## Unit 9 American Literature

### 1. Romanticism: Emerson, Hawthorne, Melville

核心句：American Romanticism stresses individual spirit, nature, imagination and the search for an American voice.

- Ralph Waldo Emerson:
  - central figure of Transcendentalism。
  - Nature is a key work。
  - believed individuals could find truth through nature, intuition and self-reliance。
  - influenced later American writers and reformers。
- Henry David Thoreau:
  - Emerson's important follower。
  - Walden records his experiment in simple living near Walden Pond。
  - Civil Disobedience argues that individuals should resist unjust government。
- Nathaniel Hawthorne:
  - wrote dark romantic fiction。
  - major work: The Scarlet Letter。
  - themes: sin, guilt, moral conflict, Puritan past。
- Herman Melville:
  - major work: Moby-Dick。
  - explores obsession, nature, evil, fate and human limits。
- Walt Whitman:
  - Leaves of Grass and Song of Myself。
  - free verse, democracy, body, self and American energy。
  - saw himself as a prototype of "The American"。

### 2. Realism: Mark Twain, Henry James, Stephen Crane

核心句：American Realism tries to present ordinary life, social truth and regional speech after the Civil War.

- Mark Twain:
  - real name Samuel Clemens。
  - one of the first major American writers strongly associated with the interior/frontier rather than East Coast literary centres。
  - used vernacular speech and humour。
  - major works: The Adventures of Tom Sawyer; Adventures of Huckleberry Finn。
  - wrote about the Mississippi River, boyhood, slavery, hypocrisy and freedom。
- Henry James:
  - wrote about the meeting of American and European cultures。
  - focused on psychology, manners and moral consciousness。
  - important for psychological realism。
- Stephen Crane:
  - associated with realism/naturalism。
  - Maggie: A Girl of the Streets shows harsh urban life。
  - The Red Badge of Courage is a major Civil War novel。

### 3. Modernism: Fitzgerald, Hemingway, Faulkner

核心句：American Modernism reflects disillusionment, experimentation and the broken promises of modern life.

- Background:
  - World War I produced disillusionment。
  - Some writers lived abroad and were called the Lost Generation。
- F. Scott Fitzgerald:
  - captured the mood of the 1920s: wealth, pleasure, restlessness and disappointment。
  - major work: The Great Gatsby。
  - theme: the American Dream becomes corrupted by money and illusion。
- Ernest Hemingway:
  - experienced war and violence。
  - style: simple, direct, restrained language; iceberg theory。
  - themes: courage, survival, loss, code hero。
  - major works: The Sun Also Rises; A Farewell to Arms; For Whom the Bell Tolls; The Old Man and the Sea。
- William Faulkner:
  - Southern writer。
  - created fictional Yoknapatawpha County。
  - themes: Southern history, race, memory, guilt, family decline。
  - techniques: stream of consciousness, multiple viewpoints, non-linear time。
  - major works: The Sound and the Fury; As I Lay Dying; Light in August; Absalom, Absalom!
  - awarded the 1949 Nobel Prize in Literature; it was announced and presented in 1950。

## Unit 10 American Education

### 1. Educational System

核心句：American education is mainly a state and local matter, aimed at universal literacy and democratic citizenship.

- Goal:
  - educate an entire national population。
  - achieve universal literacy。
  - prepare individuals for personal welfare and public life。
- Public and private schools:
  - about 90% of students attend public schools supported by taxpayers。
  - about 9-10% attend private schools in recent NCES data。
  - many private schools are run by religious groups。
- Local control:
  - the US has no national federal school system。
  - each state has its own education laws。
  - boards of education help decide spending, curriculum, teacher standards and assessment。
  - this reflects the 10th Amendment idea that education belongs largely to states and localities。
- Compulsory education:
  - all states require school attendance。
  - age limits vary by state, often to 16 or 18。
  - public education commonly covers kindergarten through grade 12, but there is no single federal school system。
- Levels:
  - elementary school: often kindergarten through grade 6 or 8。
  - secondary school: generally grades 9-12, usually high school。
  - high school diploma before college。
- Curriculum:
  - no single national curriculum。
  - elementary core: reading, writing, math, science, social studies, music, art, physical education。
  - secondary core: English, math, science, social studies, PE。
  - electives vary widely: languages, arts, business, technology, vocational subjects。
- Historical roots:
  - Puritans valued literacy so people could read the Bible。
  - Harvard College was founded in 1636 to train ministers。
  - Brown v. Board of Education in 1954 ruled racial segregation in public schools unconstitutional。
  - Sputnik in 1957 pushed the US to strengthen science and technology education。

### 2. Higher Education

核心句：American higher education is diverse, competitive and strongly linked to opportunity, but also shaped by inequality and prestige.

- Admission factors:
  - high school record。
  - teacher recommendations。
  - interviews, where used。
  - SAT/ACT scores, where required or submitted; many institutions now use test-optional policies。
- Four categories of institutions:
  - university: undergraduate colleges plus graduate/professional schools。
  - four-year college: undergraduate education, often liberal arts。
  - technical training institution: practical and professional training。
  - two-year community college: associate degrees, vocational paths or transfer to four-year colleges。
- Public and private:
  - public colleges are operated by state/local government and receive public funds。
  - private colleges depend more on tuition, endowment and donations。
  - some private colleges are religious。
- Degrees:
  - bachelor's degree often requires about 120 credits over four years。
  - graduate schools award master's and doctoral degrees。
  - liberal arts education includes history, languages, philosophy and broad intellectual training。
- Prestige:
  - Harvard, Yale, Princeton, MIT, Stanford and University of California campuses such as Berkeley or UCLA are examples of famous institutions。
  - prestige is linked to history, faculty, research, selectivity, funding and alumni networks。
- GI Bill:
  - after World War II, many veterans used government aid to attend college。
  - this changed the image of who could go to college。
- Equal opportunity:
  - women, African Americans, minorities and non-traditional students gained wider access。
  - affirmative action tried to reduce inequality; quota systems were controversial, and after the 2023 Supreme Court decision in Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard/UNC, race-conscious admissions are sharply limited。
- Recent trends:
  - more diverse college student body。
  - more women enrolled than men since the late 20th century。
  - growth of part-time and adult students。
  - online courses and open educational resources increased access to high-quality education。

## 最后冲刺：高频对比题

### UK vs US Government

- UK:
  - uncodified constitution, meaning there is no single written constitutional document。
  - parliamentary sovereignty。
  - constitutional monarchy。
  - Prime Minister comes from majority party in House of Commons。
  - fusion of executive and legislature。
- US:
  - written Constitution。
  - federalism。
  - separation of powers。
  - President elected indirectly through Electoral College。
  - strong checks and balances。

### UK vs US Education

- UK:
  - national curriculum matters more。
  - class and elite schools are a major theme。
  - GCSE and A-levels are key exams。
  - Oxford and Cambridge symbolize elite tradition。
  - Open University is important for distance and adult education。
- US:
  - education is mainly state/local。
  - no national curriculum。
  - public schools funded by taxes and governed locally。
  - high school diploma, SAT/ACT and college admission matter。
  - higher education institutions are highly diverse: universities, colleges, technical schools and community colleges。

### UK vs US National Identity

- UK:
  - identity is complicated by four constituent countries, class, region, empire and immigration。
  - British identity coexists with English, Scottish, Welsh and Northern Irish identities。
- US:
  - identity is shaped by immigration, diversity, race, religion and the idea of the American Dream。
  - melting pot suggests assimilation; salad bowl suggests diversity with distinct cultures。

## 考前一分钟名词解释模板

- Constitutional monarchy: a system in which the monarch is head of state but political power is limited by constitution, Parliament and conventions.
- Parliamentary sovereignty: the principle that Parliament is the supreme law-making authority in the British state.
- Glorious Revolution: the 1688-1689 replacement of James II by William and Mary, leading to the Bill of Rights and constitutional monarchy.
- Norman Conquest: William the Conqueror's 1066 conquest of England, which changed England's ruling class, language and political structure.
- Thatcherism: Margaret Thatcher's free-market policies, including privatisation, monetarism, tax cuts, reduced public spending and weaker trade unions.
- Romanticism: a literary movement emphasizing imagination, emotion, nature, individuality and freedom.
- Mayflower Compact: an agreement signed by the Pilgrims in 1620 to create civil government for the common good in their new colony.
- Bill of Rights: the first ten amendments to the US Constitution, protecting individual liberties.
- Checks and balances: a system in which each branch of US government can limit the power of the others.
- Electoral College: the body of electors through which the US president is formally elected.
- Melting pot: a metaphor for immigrant groups assimilating into one American culture.
- Salad bowl: a metaphor for groups keeping distinct cultural identities within a diverse society.
- Transcendentalism: an American Romantic movement led by Emerson and Thoreau, stressing intuition, nature and self-reliance.
- Lost Generation: post-WWI writers such as Fitzgerald and Hemingway who expressed disillusionment with modern society.
- Community college: a two-year American higher education institution offering associate degrees, vocational training or transfer routes.
