# 大学英语期末作文速成复习：英美社会与文化

适用题型：200 词以上，300 词左右的 essay。  
目标：考场上能快速确定主题、套入结构、写出有事实支撑的短文。

## 一、最稳作文结构

### 1. 四段式

建议写 230-270 词，四段最稳：

1. Introduction：点题 + 总观点，约 40-50 词。
2. Body 1：第一个原因/特点 + 例子，约 60-70 词。
3. Body 2：第二、第三个原因/特点 + 例子，约 90-110 词。
4. Conclusion：总结 + 个人评价，约 35-45 词。

### 2. 万能开头

可直接背：

> When we talk about [topic], we are not only talking about a single fact, but also about history, culture and social values. In my opinion, [topic] is important because it helps us understand how Britain/the United States has developed and how people there think about society today.

更短版本：

> [Topic] is an important part of British/American society and culture. It reflects the country's history, values and social development. In this essay, I will discuss it from three aspects: history, main features and influence.

### 3. 万能结尾

可直接背：

> In conclusion, [topic] is more than a textbook fact. It shows the connection between the past and the present. By studying it, we can better understand British/American society and also compare it with our own culture.

更自然版本：

> To sum up, [topic] has shaped Britain/the United States in many ways. It is useful for us because it helps explain both the achievements and the problems of the country.

## 二、考场万能句型

### 1. 引出观点

- This topic is important because it shows how history influences modern society.
- One important feature of this topic is that it connects tradition with change.
- It is not enough to see it as a simple fact; we should also understand its social meaning.
- From my point of view, this is one of the most useful topics for understanding British/American culture.

### 2. 展开原因

- To begin with, history plays an important role.
- Another important reason is related to politics and social values.
- In addition, this topic also has a strong influence on people's daily life.
- Finally, it helps us understand the identity of the country.

### 3. 举例

- For example, the United Kingdom is made up of England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.
- For instance, the American Constitution created a federal system with checks and balances.
- A good example is the British monarchy, because the monarch reigns but does not rule.
- A typical example is American immigration, which has made the United States a diverse society.

### 4. 对比

- Compared with Britain, the United States puts more emphasis on a written Constitution and separation of powers.
- Unlike the United States, Britain has a constitutional monarchy and an uncodified constitution.
- While Britain has a stronger national curriculum, American education is mainly controlled by states and local communities.
- Both countries value democracy, but they organize political power in different ways.

### 5. 评价

- This system has both advantages and problems.
- It provides stability, but it may also create inequality.
- It encourages freedom and opportunity, but it cannot solve all social problems.
- Therefore, we should understand it in a balanced way.

## 三、高概率主题与可套用观点

### 1. British Identity

核心观点：

> British identity is complicated because it is shaped by four constituent countries, regional differences, class, empire and immigration.

可写要点：

- The UK includes England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.
- British identity coexists with English, Scottish, Welsh and Northern Irish identities.
- Scotland and Wales have strong local cultures and historical memories.
- Immigration from Commonwealth countries made Britain a multiracial society.
- Class and regional differences still influence education, accent, jobs and lifestyle.

适合题目：

- What do you know about British identity?
- Why is British identity complicated?
- Write about one feature of British society.

### 2. British Government / Monarchy / Parliament

核心观点：

> Britain is a parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy. The monarch is the head of state, but real political power belongs to Parliament, the Prime Minister and the Cabinet.

可写要点：

- The monarch reigns but does not rule.
- Parliament consists of the monarch, the House of Lords and the House of Commons.
- The House of Commons is elected and is the real centre of political power.
- Parliamentary sovereignty means Parliament is the supreme law-making authority.
- Supporters see monarchy as a symbol of tradition and unity; critics see it as hereditary and undemocratic.

适合题目：

- What is constitutional monarchy?
- What do you know about the British Parliament?
- Is the British monarchy still important?

### 3. UK vs US Government

核心观点：

> Britain and the United States are both democracies, but Britain has parliamentary sovereignty and constitutional monarchy, while the United States has a written Constitution, federalism and separation of powers.

可写要点：

- UK: uncodified constitution, Parliament, Prime Minister, Cabinet.
- US: written Constitution, President, Congress, Supreme Court.
- UK executive and legislature are closely connected.
- US power is divided among three branches.
- Both systems try to limit power and protect democracy.

适合题目：

- Compare British and American political systems.
- What are the main features of the US Constitution?
- Write about checks and balances.

### 4. American Identity / Immigration / Diversity

核心观点：

> The United States is a nation of immigrants, and its national identity is shaped by diversity, opportunity and conflict.

可写要点：

- Native Americans lived in North America before Europeans arrived.
- Europeans, Africans, Asians and Latin Americans shaped American society.
- The melting pot stresses assimilation; the salad bowl stresses diversity.
- Immigration brought labour, culture and energy.
- It also caused debates about race, jobs, borders and national identity.

适合题目：

- Why is the United States called a nation of immigrants?
- What do you know about American diversity?
- Explain the melting pot and salad bowl.

### 5. American History: Revolution / Civil War

核心观点：

> American history is marked by the pursuit of freedom and equality, but also by contradictions such as slavery, racial conflict and exclusion.

可写要点：

- American Revolution: no taxation without representation, independence, republic.
- Declaration of Independence: natural rights, equality, government by consent.
- Civil War: slavery, states' rights, Union vs Confederacy.
- Results: Union preserved, slavery abolished, Reconstruction began.
- Contradiction: equality was an ideal, but many groups were excluded.

适合题目：

- What is the significance of the American Revolution?
- Write about the Civil War.
- How did American history shape American values?

### 6. Education: UK vs US

核心观点：

> British education reflects class and national standards, while American education reflects local control, diversity and equal opportunity.

可写要点：

- UK: public schools like Eton are actually private elite schools.
- UK: GCSE, A-levels, National Curriculum, Oxbridge.
- US: education is mainly a state and local matter.
- US: no single national curriculum; public schools are funded by taxes.
- Higher education in both countries offers opportunity but also shows inequality and prestige.

适合题目：

- Compare British and American education.
- What do you know about British education?
- What are the features of American higher education?

### 7. Economy / Industrial Revolution

核心观点：

> Britain was the first country to industrialise, and the Industrial Revolution made it powerful but also created urban and social problems.

可写要点：

- Britain had coal, iron, capital, markets and political stability.
- Textiles, coal, iron, steam power and railways developed quickly.
- Industrialization created factories, cities, middle class and working class.
- It also caused poverty, child labour, bad working conditions and class conflict.
- Modern Britain and the United States are both service-dominated economies.

适合题目：

- Write about the Industrial Revolution.
- What are the effects of industrialization?
- What do you know about British/American economy?

### 8. Literature Fallback

核心观点：

> Literature is useful because it shows the values, conflicts and social problems of a country.

可写要点：

- Shakespeare: human nature, ambition, love, power, jealousy.
- Romantic poets: nature, imagination, emotion, freedom.
- Dickens: Victorian poverty, children, workhouses, social injustice.
- Mark Twain: Mississippi River, boyhood, slavery, freedom, humour.
- Fitzgerald: American Dream, money, illusion, disappointment.

适合题目：

- Write about a book by a British writer.
- Write about a writer you like.
- What can literature teach us about society?

## 四、万能题目改写方法

- American education

开头可写：

> I would like to write about [topic], because it is a good window into British/American society.

结构：

2. It shaped national identity.

结构：

2. Admit one opposite view.
3. Explain your own stronger view.

## 五、可直接背的范文

### Essay 1: British Identity

**Topic: Why is British identity complicated?**

British identity is an important topic because it helps us understand the history and society of the United Kingdom. In my opinion, British identity is complicated because it is shaped by different nations, regions, classes and historical experiences.

To begin with, the United Kingdom is not the same as England. It is made up of England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. People may call themselves British, but they may also strongly identify as English, Scottish, Welsh or Northern Irish. For example, Scotland had a long history as an independent kingdom before the Union with England in 1707. Wales also keeps its own cultural identity, especially through the Welsh language.

In addition, British identity is influenced by class, region and immigration. London is the political and financial centre, but many people outside London feel that regional differences are important. Class can also be seen in education, accent and lifestyle. After the Second World War, immigration from Commonwealth countries made Britain a multiracial society, so modern British identity is more diverse than before.

In conclusion, British identity is not a simple label. It is a mixture of national traditions, local cultures and modern diversity. By studying it, we can better understand both the unity and the differences within the United Kingdom.

### Essay 2: British Monarchy

**Topic: What do you know about the British monarchy?**

The British monarchy is one of the most famous institutions in the United Kingdom. It is important not only because of history and tradition, but also because it shows how Britain has developed into a constitutional monarchy.

In Britain, the monarch is the head of state, but real political power is exercised by the elected government. A common sentence is that the monarch "reigns but does not rule." The monarch formally appoints the Prime Minister, gives Royal Assent to bills, opens Parliament and represents the nation on ceremonial occasions. However, the Prime Minister, the Cabinet and Parliament make the real political decisions.

The monarchy has both supporters and critics. Supporters believe that it provides stability, tradition and a non-party symbol of national unity. It can also attract public attention and tourism. Critics argue that it is hereditary, costly and not fully democratic. Therefore, the monarchy is not only a political institution, but also a topic of public debate.

In conclusion, the British monarchy is useful for understanding British culture and politics. It connects the past with the present. Although it no longer governs the country, it still plays a symbolic and ceremonial role in modern Britain.

### Essay 3: UK and US Government

**Topic: Compare the British and American political systems.**

Britain and the United States are both democratic countries, but their political systems are quite different. The British system is based on parliamentary sovereignty and constitutional monarchy, while the American system is based on a written Constitution, federalism and separation of powers.

In Britain, Parliament is the supreme law-making authority. Parliament includes the monarch, the House of Lords and the House of Commons, but the House of Commons is the real centre of political power because its members are elected. The Prime Minister is usually the leader of the party with a majority in the House of Commons. This means the executive and the legislature are closely connected.

In the United States, the Constitution is the supreme law. Power is divided among three branches: Congress, the President and the Supreme Court. Congress makes laws, the President enforces laws, and the courts interpret the Constitution. The system of checks and balances is designed to prevent any one branch from becoming too powerful. The President is not directly elected by popular vote, but through the Electoral College.

In conclusion, both systems try to protect democracy, but they do so in different ways. Britain values parliamentary tradition, while the United States emphasizes constitutional limits and divided power.

### Essay 4: American Immigration and Diversity

**Topic: Why is the United States called a nation of immigrants?**

The United States is often called a nation of immigrants because immigration has shaped its population, economy and national identity. Except for Native Americans, most Americans are descendants of people who came from other parts of the world, either voluntarily or by force.

In early American history, many settlers came from Europe, while Africans were brought by force as slaves. In the nineteenth century, large numbers of Irish, Germans, Scandinavians and Chinese immigrants arrived. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, many people came from Southern and Eastern Europe. After 1965, immigration from Asia and Latin America increased greatly because the national-origin quota system was removed.

Immigration brought labour, skills and cultural diversity to the United States. It helped build railways, cities, farms and industries. It also created the idea of the "melting pot", which means different groups become part of one American nation. However, the "salad bowl" may be more accurate, because many groups keep their own languages, religions and customs.

In conclusion, immigration is central to American identity. It has created opportunity and energy, but it has also caused conflict over race, jobs and national belonging. To understand America, we must understand immigration.

### Essay 5: British and American Education

**Topic: Compare British and American education.**

British and American education systems share the goal of preparing young people for society, but they have different features. British education is often linked with class and national standards, while American education is mainly shaped by state and local control.

In Britain, education has long reflected class differences. Famous public schools such as Eton and Harrow are actually private, fee-paying independent schools, and they are connected with elite social networks. At the same time, state education expanded greatly after the 1944 Education Act. Today, students usually take GCSEs at about sixteen and A-levels before university. The National Curriculum also gives schools a common set of subjects and standards.

In the United States, education is mainly a state and local matter. There is no single national school system or national curriculum. Public schools are supported by taxpayers and governed locally, while private schools also exist. American higher education is very diverse, including universities, four-year colleges, technical institutions and community colleges. It offers many opportunities, but it is also influenced by cost, prestige and inequality.

In conclusion, both countries see education as important for personal success and citizenship. Britain shows the influence of tradition and class, while the United States shows diversity and local control.

### Essay 6: Industrial Revolution

**Topic: What were the effects of the Industrial Revolution in Britain?**

The Industrial Revolution was one of the most important events in British history. Britain was the first country to industrialise, and this change made it economically powerful. However, industrialization also created many social problems.

There were several reasons why the Industrial Revolution began in Britain. Britain had rich coal and iron resources, capital from trade and empire, a large market and political stability. New inventions, especially steam power, changed production. Textiles, coal, iron, steam engines and railways became major industries.

The Industrial Revolution had great effects. The factory system replaced much traditional hand production, and many people moved from the countryside to cities. Industrial cities such as Manchester grew quickly. Railways and steam engines also changed transport and daily life. A stronger middle class and working class appeared, and Britain became a dominant economic power in the nineteenth century. At the same time, workers often suffered from poverty, child labour, long hours and bad working conditions. Class conflict became more serious.

In conclusion, the Industrial Revolution brought both progress and pain. It helped create modern Britain and modern capitalism, but it also reminded people that economic growth must be considered together with social justice.

### Essay 7: A British Book

**Topic: Write about a book by a British writer that you like.**

One British book I like is *Oliver Twist* by Charles Dickens. Dickens was one of the greatest novelists of the nineteenth century, and he was also a powerful social critic. I like this book because it tells an interesting story and also exposes serious social problems in Victorian Britain.

The novel tells the story of Oliver, a poor orphan who grows up in a workhouse and later suffers in London. Through Oliver's experience, Dickens shows the poverty, cruelty and injustice faced by children and the poor. The workhouse, the criminal world and the cold attitude of society all make the reader feel sympathy for weak people.

What impresses me most is Dickens's ability to create vivid characters. Oliver is innocent and kind, while characters such as Fagin and Bill Sikes show the dark side of society. At the same time, the novel is not only sad. It also has suspense, humour and hope. Dickens wants readers to care about social reform and human kindness.

In conclusion, *Oliver Twist* is more than a story about one child. It helps us understand Victorian society and the moral responsibility of literature. That is why I think it is worth reading.

## 七、快速扩字法

如果考场要求更接近 250-300 词，不要重写结构，只在 Body 段加 2-3 句：

- 加历史背景：Historically, this topic developed over a long period, so it cannot be understood only from the present.
- 加具体例子：For example, this can be seen in important institutions, laws and public debates.
- 加平衡评价：However, this system also has weaknesses, so we should not describe it as perfect.
- 加比较句：Compared with China or other countries, this feature shows a different way of organizing society.

万能扩字句：

> This example shows that culture is not separate from politics and history. It is closely connected with people's daily life and national identity.
